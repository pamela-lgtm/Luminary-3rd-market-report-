Section Two

Why showings haven't produced applications yet

The numbers above are raw counts — every inquiry and every showing, with nothing filtered out. We report them that way on purpose, and it means some of that activity will never produce an application or even feedback for us to pass along.

Becky works every inquiry personally, by phone and by text, and every prospect is pointed to our Resident Selection Criteria before a showing is arranged.

That is where a number of them stop. Once a prospect reads the criteria and recognizes they would not qualify — credit that does not meet the standard, income, a restricted breed, a move-in date that does not work — they simply stop replying. They are not declining the home. They have decided not to spend their time on an application they know will not be approved, and they see no reason to write back and tell us so.

What that does and does not mean

It does not mean the property fell short. Nobody has raised the rent, the photographs or the home itself as an objection.

It does not mean we stopped following up. Every inquiry is called, texted and invited to tour, and we follow up again before treating a lead as closed.

It does mean the raw numbers overstate the qualified pool. What matters is not how many people toured, but how many are qualified, available on your timeline, and still interested after a real conversation.

We would far rather a prospect step away at that stage than submit an application we have to decline — and far rather either of those than place a resident who should not have been approved in the first place.

Section Three

The two pieces of feedback we received

We want you to see both, including the one we are recommending you act on and the one we are flagging as a warning.

Ceiling fans — worth considering

A prospect asked whether the owner would be installing ceiling fans. That particular renter did not qualify, so the question did not cost us an application. But it is worth raising with you anyway, because it is not an unusual expectation.

In Florida, renters generally expect ceiling fans in the bedrooms and the main living area. They are a comfort item and an energy-cost item, and their absence is the kind of thing a renter notices on a tour without necessarily mentioning it to us.

If you are open to it, we will get quotes and bring you the numbers. It is a modest, one-time cost that stays with the property, and it removes an objection we may be hearing silently from prospects who tour and move on.

The second prospect — a clear decline

A second prospect did not qualify under our criteria, and the conversation raised two significant concerns beyond that.

He asked for our advice on how he and his family could get out of their current lease, citing mold, and then asked whether the owner of your property would pay half the cost of doing so.

We did not advise him. We are not a party to his existing lease, we have no way to verify the circumstances, and it would be inappropriate for us to counsel a resident on exiting an agreement with another landlord.

Beyond the qualification issue, the request itself tells us a great deal. A prospect looking for a landlord to underwrite their exit from a previous obligation, before a lease has even been discussed, is telling us how the next tenancy would likely go. We declined and moved on, and we would make the same call again.

We share both because it is easy to look at showings without applications and assume something is wrong with the home or the price. In this case what it reflects is our screening standard doing exactly what it is meant to do.

Section Six

Our recommendation

Hold at $2,995

No price change this week. The showing volume tells us the number is right, and reducing a price that is already generating traffic would give away rent for no reason.

When a price is wrong, the market goes quiet. Ours is not quiet.

One thing to consider: ceiling fans

This is the only decision we are putting in front of you this week, and it is entirely optional. If you are open to installing fans in the bedrooms and main living area, we will gather quotes and send them to you before anything is scheduled.

It is a modest, one-time improvement that stays with the property, and in this market it removes a comfort expectation that Florida renters generally have.

We also want to set a fair expectation. Showings without applications for one week is normal, particularly when several prospects do not meet our screening standard. Showings without applications for three or four consecutive weeks would be a different signal, and if we see that we will tell you plainly and bring you a recommendation rather than wait.
